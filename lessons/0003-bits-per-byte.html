Bits Per Byte (bpb)

Lesson 3 · How to measure byte-level model quality fairly

Why This Matters

In Lesson 2 you ran experiments and saw val_bpb: BPE = 1.59, byte-level = 1.92. But what does that number actually mean? And can you compare it directly to BPE's perplexity?

bpb is the metric that lets you compare a byte-level model against a BPE model fairly — exactly the comparison you need to decide whether byte-level input is worth the quality trade-off for your synthetic story generator.

The Core Idea

bpb (bits per byte) is the average number of bits the model needs to encode each byte of text, given its predictions. Lower is better — fewer bits means the model is less surprised by the data.

It's the byte-level analog of perplexity. Where perplexity asks "how many tokens is the model choosing among?", bpb asks "how many bits per byte does the model need?"

bpb = −log₂ P(x) / |bytes| = cross-entropy (bits) ÷ bytes

In practice, training frameworks report cross-entropy loss in nats (natural log). For a byte-level model, every token is exactly one byte, so:

bpb = loss_nats ÷ ln(2)

Worked Example — Your Own Results

From your Lesson 2 experiments (Apple Silicon, 5-minute budget):

Convert bpb to "per-byte perplexity" — how many bytes the model is effectively choosing among at each position:

perplexity per byte = 2^bpb
  • BPE: 2^1.59 ≈ 3.0 bytes
  • byte-level: 2^1.92 ≈ 3.8 bytes

Interpretation: at each position, the byte-level model is about as uncertain as picking among ~4 bytes; BPE picks among ~3. The byte-level model is slightly more surprised — consistent with it having to learn word structure from raw bytes.

Sanity check: a model that guesses uniformly at random among 256 bytes needs log₂(256) = 8 bpb. Your 1.92 is far below 8, so the model has clearly learned real structure.

Why Not Just Use Perplexity?

Perplexity is normalized per token. But BPE tokens and bytes are different units: one BPE token can be 1 byte ("a") or several bytes (an emoji). Comparing raw perplexity across tokenizers is like comparing miles-per-gallon to liters-per-100km — the units differ.

bpb normalizes by bytes, which is intrinsic to the text and tokenizer-independent. That's why Gao et al. (2020, The Pile) proposed bpb as the standard way to compare models with different tokenizations.

In your evaluate_bpb, the byte-level case is simple: every target is one byte, so no byte-length lookup is needed. That's the change you made when you removed the tokenizer — evaluate_bpb no longer needs a token_bytes lookup because every target byte has length 1.

What bpb Tells You About Your Mission

  • bpb lets you compare byte-level vs BPE model quality on equal terms. You can't compare their raw perplexities, but you can compare their bpb.
  • Lower bpb = better compression = better next-byte prediction = generally better downstream generation.
  • Byte-level trades ~0.33 bpb of quality for ~2.4× more training steps per minute and half the VRAM — often worth it on memory-constrained Apple Silicon.
